ShieldWise is an awareness and educational project — it does not itself file complaints with police or government authorities. For emergencies unrelated to cybercrime, dial 100 for local police.

  1. 1

    Call 1930 immediately

    India's national cybercrime helpline. It can freeze a fraudulent transaction within the "golden hour" if you call fast enough.

  2. 2

    File a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in

    Keep screenshots, transaction IDs, and the sender's number or UPI ID ready before you start.

  3. 3

    Contact your bank's fraud helpline

    Ask them to block the card/UPI handle and flag the transaction for reversal.

  4. 4

    Change passwords and enable 2FA

    Start with email and banking apps — those unlock everything else.

  5. 5

    Report the account on the platform itself

    WhatsApp, Instagram and email providers all have a direct "report scam/phishing" option — it helps stop the next person.

Before You Report

Keep these details ready before filing your complaint — based on the official cybercrime complaint requirements. Having them ready speeds up the process significantly.

📅

Incident Details · Mandatory

  • • Date and time of the incident
  • • Incident description (minimum 200 characters)
🪪

Identity Proof · Mandatory

  • • Voter ID, Driving Licence, Passport, PAN or Aadhaar
  • • Soft copy — JPEG, JPG or PNG
  • • Maximum file size: 5 MB
💳

Financial Fraud Details · If applicable

  • • Bank / wallet / merchant name
  • • 12-digit Transaction ID / UTR number
  • • Date of transaction and fraud amount
📎

Evidence · Mandatory

  • • Screenshots, transaction proof, messages or emails
  • • Any other relevant documents
  • • Maximum 10 MB per file
🔎

Suspect Information · Optional

  • • Suspected website URL / social media handle
  • • Mobile number, email ID or bank account, if known
  • • Address, photograph, or any identifying document
Never share your OTP, PIN, password, CVV or full banking credentials with ShieldWise, this checklist, or anyone else — not even to "prove" what happened. Official complaint channels never need these either.

Checking or reporting a suspicious number, email or website

ShieldWise does not run its own lookup database — that would risk showing an unverified or fabricated result, which is worse than no result at all. The National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP) runs two real, official facilities for exactly this:

🔎 Suspect Repository (search)

Search a mobile number, email, bank account, or URL against NCRP's database of reported cybercriminal identifiers.

🚩 Report Suspect (submit)

Report a suspicious website URL, WhatsApp/Telegram handle, phone number, email, SMS header, social media URL, deepfake, or app — even before any money is lost.

Both are run directly by India's Ministry of Home Affairs — ShieldWise only links to them.

Fraud trends worth knowing about

These are patterns widely reported by banks, telecom providers and law-enforcement advisories — not a live news feed. This list is updated periodically, not automatically; use the links below for what's happening right now.

"Digital arrest" scams

Callers impersonate police/CBI/customs on a video call, claim a parcel or your Aadhaar is linked to a crime, and pressure victims to transfer money to "clear their name." No Indian law-enforcement agency conducts an arrest or investigation over a video call.

Fake trading/investment apps

Victims are added to groups showing fabricated profit dashboards, then blocked from withdrawing until they pay a fake "tax" or "release fee." Always check if an advisor/platform is SEBI-registered.

AI voice-cloning calls

A short public video/voice clip is enough to clone a family member's voice convincingly for a fake emergency call. Covered in detail in the Detector's "Listen to a call" tab.

UPI "scan to receive" refund tricks

Scammers claim they overpaid and ask victims to scan a QR code to "return" the difference — scanning + PIN entry always sends money out, never receives it.

COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT PROGRAM

About this awareness program

This platform was built as a Community Engagement Project to reduce phishing, scam and fraud victimisation through practical, self-paced awareness training.

Objectives

  • • Improve recognition of common scam patterns across digital channels
  • • Give people a concrete, repeatable checklist instead of vague caution
  • • Measure awareness change with a short pre/post survey
  • • Provide a clear, fast path to reporting when something already went wrong

Target audience

  • • College students and first-time UPI/banking app users
  • • Job seekers and freelancers targeted by fake offers
  • • First-time investors approached via social media "tips"
  • • Families and older relatives, often targeted by voice-based scams

Methodology

  1. STEP 1

    Research common scam patterns and real case reports

  2. STEP 2

    Build an interactive awareness platform (this site)

  3. STEP 3

    Run a pre-awareness survey with the target group

  4. STEP 4

    Deliver the program, then re-survey to measure change

Expected outcome

A measurable rise in participants' ability to correctly identify scam messages and describe the right reporting steps, benchmarked using the survey and quiz results collected during the program.

PROJECT INFORMATION

About ShieldWise

ShieldWise is a Community Engagement Project (CEP) built to make phishing, scam and fraud awareness practical rather than theoretical — through an interactive detector, realistic decision scenarios, and a self-graded quiz, backed by a measurable pre/post survey.

Built with

HTML, Tailwind CSS, vanilla JavaScript and GSAP — entirely free, open-source front-end technology with zero paid APIs. Hosted for free on Vercel, Netlify or GitHub Pages. Badges, checklist answers and other progress are stored locally via localStorage, and survey data via a free Google Form.

Your data and privacy

  • • The message scanner, URL checker, and vishing simulator run entirely in your browser. Nothing you paste into them is sent to ShieldWise, or to any third party — there is no backend and no external analysis API involved.
  • • No live domain-reputation or threat-intelligence service is used, because doing so safely would require a backend to protect an API key, which this project intentionally doesn't have. Where that limits the checkers, the tool says so directly rather than faking a result.
  • • Your badges, quiz history, checklist answers, and family safety card are saved only in your own browser's local storage — never uploaded anywhere. Clearing your browser data clears them.
  • • Survey responses go directly to Google's own Forms/Sheets infrastructure. The Survey page shows a live, aggregated chart of anonymous answer counts (e.g. "6 people chose option A") from a separate, non-sensitive summary sheet — no names, emails or free-text answers are ever published or displayed.
  • • ShieldWise never asks for and should never be given your OTP, PIN, password, CVV or full banking credentials, even to "demonstrate" a scam.

Cyber Safety Quick Reference — Print & Keep

🚩 8 Signs of a Scam

  • "Act now" — urgent deadlines that don't allow time to verify
  • Asks you to share an OTP, PIN, password or CVV
  • Asks you to scan a QR code to "receive" money
  • Claims your account will be blocked or suspended
  • A job or investment that pays without real work or risk
  • Asks you to keep it secret from family or friends
  • Asks you to install a remote-access app
  • A too-good-to-be-true prize you never entered for

✅ Golden Rules

  1. Never share an OTP, PIN, password or CVV with anyone — ever.
  2. Verify independently: call the bank/company's official number, not one from a message.
  3. A PIN is only for paying — never for receiving money.
  4. Hang up and call back on a known number for any "emergency" call.
